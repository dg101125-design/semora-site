04 / How the work is done

The monthly cycle, and what closes each step

An agency will show you the result and keep the working. Here is the working: what runs every month, and what each step has to produce before it counts as done.

Monthly advertising report How it is produced

  1. 01

    Agree the number before the budget

    We write down the enquiry or pipeline figure the spend has to move, and the date we will read it. Access to the ad accounts and analytics comes first, in your name.

    Closes on one agreed number, with today's figure beside it.

  2. 02

    Build the account so waste is visible

    Campaigns, ad groups and negative keywords are structured so the search-term report can be read at a glance. An account nobody can read is an account nobody can improve.

    Closes on a structure and a search-term list you can audit.

  3. 03

    Spend, then cut

    Every week the search terms are reviewed, negatives are added, creative is rotated, and budget moves toward whatever is producing enquiries you would answer.

    Closes on a dated change log for the month.

  4. 04

    Report against the number

    Enquiries, qualified pipeline and new clients on the first line. Impressions, clicks and cost per click sit underneath as the explanation for the line above them.

    Closes on next month, held against this one.

Why it holds

  • Each platform counts the conversions it can attribute to itself, on its own window. Added together those totals can come to more conversions than you had clients, so the report starts from your CRM.
  • Every change carries its date, so a month that improved can be told apart from a month the market moved.
  • Where a channel is not returning, that goes in the report. Moving the budget quietly is how a bad quarter stays invisible.

06 / The limits

Five things we will not do with your advertising

Each of these is a standing rule here. They are printed so you can hold us to them.

  1. Put your media spend on our account

    You pay the platforms direct. Money passing through an agency is money whose margin you cannot see.

  2. Price on performance alone

    A share-of-result fee needs a reliable baseline, a fixed floor, a cap, one agreed source of truth and terms a lawyer wrote. Without those five it is a bet, and you carry it.

  3. Buy an email list

    Australian spam law requires consent. A purchased list is a fine waiting for a date, and the reply rate was never worth it.

  4. Publish at volume without a reader

    Every piece answers a question somebody actually asks, gets checked for accuracy, and is approved by a person before it goes live.

  5. Promise a position or a return

    No platform sells one. We commit to the work, the quantities and the reporting, and we publish the result either way.

Four numbers.One is revenue.

  3. Enquiries

07 / Questions we get asked

Frequently asked questions

Who pays the platforms, you or us?

You do, direct. Your card sits on your own Google Ads, Meta and LinkedIn accounts and the platform bills you for the media. Our fee is separate and published, so it stays the same whether your budget goes up or down that month.

Do we own the ad accounts?

Yes. Accounts are opened in your business name and we work inside them with access you grant and can withdraw. If the engagement ends, the campaign history, the audiences and the conversion data stay where they are.

How much should we spend?

Enough to buy a readable month. A budget that produces two or three conversions gives nobody a signal to act on, which means the first month is spent learning rather than earning. We set the figure against the value of one client to you, and we write down what it has to return before it starts.

Why does LinkedIn cost more than Google?

Because you are bidding for a named job title rather than for a search, and everyone else who wants that title is in the same auction. LinkedIn publishes no rate card, so the real cost of a click is discovered in your first month rather than quoted in a proposal. It earns its price when one client is worth a great deal and the buying committee is several people.

Can Google spend more than our daily budget?

On any given day, yes. Google publishes the rule: a campaign can spend up to twice its average daily budget on a busy day, and over a month you are never charged more than 30.4 times that daily average. The monthly figure is the one to budget against. We set daily budgets from the monthly number you signed off, not the other way round.

Can you guarantee a cost per lead?

No. The auction price moves with your competitors, your season and your own conversion rate, and nobody controls all three. What we commit to is the work, the published quantities, the tracking being correct before money runs, and a report that shows the figure whether it improved or not.

Is this priced?

Yes, in figures, on the quotation page and the homepage. A quote here is arithmetic on published numbers, and the same numbers go to everyone.
