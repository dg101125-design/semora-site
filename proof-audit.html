The instrument

The eleven surfaces,
and the question each one answers.

A buyer of expert advice checks these, roughly in this order, before they ever speak to you. Each is scored 0–4 with the evidence recorded beside it. Score yourself first, free →

The order of work

Two weeks.
Ninety minutes of yours.

You give us two things: one 90-minute working session, and your last five proposals. Nothing else is asked of you, and nothing waits on your team.

Then, in order: the eleven surfaces scored against evidence · your baseline established — win rate on qualified enquiries, average fee, founder-hours per win · your last five proposals read against the ones that won · the three competitors you name, scored on the same instrument — and if that list is the wrong one, we say so and why · the three surfaces to fix, ranked, with the reason each sits where it does.

It ends with a 60-minute findings session, recorded, and a written report. The recording and the report are yours.

Why it is paid

A free audit is a sales call in a costume.

Specialists can tell. Charging makes it an engagement rather than a pitch, which is what makes the findings usable — and it filters for people who are actually going to act on them.

You keep the report whether or not you hire us.

The $3,000 is credited in full against any engagement started within 90 days. Work with us and the diagnostic costs nothing; walk away and you still hold a scored baseline and a ranked list of what to fix.

What it is not

Not a strategy.
Not a proposal.

It measures where you stand. It does not decide your position, write your messaging or build anything — that is SIGNAL / and / HAUS, priced separately and published in the same detail.

It is also optional. An engagement can start at SIGNAL / instead, and the baseline is set there in week one. The rule we do not bend is the one underneath it: no work without a baseline — otherwise nobody can say at 90 days whether anything moved.
