05 / Questions we get asked

Frequently asked questions

Who pays the platforms?

You do, direct. Your card sits on your own Google Ads, Meta and LinkedIn accounts and the platform bills you for the media. Our fee is separate and published, so it stays the same whether your budget goes up or down that month.

Do we own the ad accounts?

Yes. Accounts are opened in your business name and we work inside them with access you grant and can withdraw. If the engagement ends, the campaign history, the audiences and the conversion data stay where they are.

How much should we spend?

Enough to buy a readable month. A budget that produces two or three conversions gives nobody a signal to act on, which means the first month is spent learning rather than earning. We set the figure against the value of one client to you, and we write down what it has to return before it starts.

Why does LinkedIn cost more than Google?

Because you are bidding for a named job title rather than for a search, and everyone else who wants that title is in the same auction. LinkedIn publishes no rate card, so the real cost of a click is discovered in your first month rather than quoted in a proposal. It earns its price when one client is worth a great deal and the buying committee is several people.

Can Google spend more than our daily budget?

On any given day, yes. Google publishes the rule: a campaign can spend up to twice its average daily budget on a busy day, and over a month you are never charged more than 30.4 times that daily average. The monthly figure is the one to budget against. We set daily budgets from the monthly number you signed off, not the other way round.

Can you guarantee a cost per lead?

No. The auction price moves with your competitors, your season and your own conversion rate, and nobody controls all three. What we commit to is the work, the published quantities, the tracking being correct before money runs, and a report that shows the figure whether it improved or not.

Is this priced?

Five of the six carry a figure on the quotation page. Content management is scoped with you before it is priced. A quote here is arithmetic on published numbers, and the same numbers go to everyone.
